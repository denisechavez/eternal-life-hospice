---
name: Service-page editorial decisions
description: Owner-approved service-brief wording exceptions and approval boundaries.
---

Omit “Phone access does not mean a nurse stays continuously at the bedside.”
from the 24/7 Nurse Access service-page introduction.

**Why:** The owner explicitly removed this sentence while approving the other
recommended service-brief revisions.

**How to apply:** Use the approved introduction describing phone support,
assessment and clinically needed visits. This is a wording decision, not a
claim that continuous bedside nursing is generally provided. Do not generalize
the removal into deleting clinical distinctions from unrelated existing pages.
The approval does not waive the brief's outstanding clinical, billing or
operational checks and does not authorize publication.

Service-page photographs should not carry visible illustrative-scene footnotes
or caption overlays.

**Why:** The owner explicitly requested removing these notes while refining
the Services overview's appearance.

**How to apply:** Keep service imagery clean and retain truthful descriptive
alt text. Removing an overlay is not permission to represent generated scenes
as actual Eternal patients or staff.

Do not show the Home / Services breadcrumb bar on the Services overview
or any individual service page.

**Why:** The owner repeated that the Home bar was still visible on individual
service pages and expanded the removal beyond the overview.

**How to apply:** Let every service hero follow the shared header directly,
without a breadcrumb or preview-notice strip between them. Preserve accurate
breadcrumb structured data and the server-side preview/publication boundary.

Service-page photographs should feel naturally integrated into the page,
not like shaped cutouts.

**Why:** The owner rejected the cutout appearance of the arched photograph
and selected the edge-to-edge treatment after comparing alternatives on the
canvas.

**How to apply:** Use natural-color rectangular hero photography meeting
the right, top and bottom edges beside the plum text field, with a full-width
stack on mobile. Do not restore arches or offset gold backing. The owner
authorized this treatment across the Services overview and service-page suite,
but not publication.

Hospice clinicians in illustrative service imagery should have believable
professional details, including a clipped photo-ID badge and a stethoscope
when appropriate to their role.

**Why:** The owner explicitly identified these ordinary details as important
to making care imagery feel true to life. The owner also flagged a bereavement
scene where two people looked like the same person duplicated.

**How to apply:** Preserve natural skin texture, faces, lighting and believable
equipment placement. Do not give medical equipment to patients, relatives or
nonclinical support workers. Keep existing stethoscopes rather than duplicating
them. Badges must not imply verified real staff identities or invented legible
credentials; these scenes remain illustrative, not actual patient encounters.
Prefer calm, reassuring care moments over glum patient portraits; an
over-the-shoulder view is appropriate when an expression distracts from the
care message. Do not replace natural expressions with forced cheerful poses.
People sharing a scene should have clearly distinguishable faces and
appearances rather than looking like near-duplicate models.

Represent nurses with both men and women in the service imagery rather than
defaulting to an entirely female nursing cast.

**Why:** The owner explicitly requested including a couple of male nurses
alongside the female nurses.

**How to apply:** Include a natural mix without changing patient or family
members to achieve it. This is permission for representative illustrative
imagery, not evidence about the identities or makeup of Eternal's real staff.
