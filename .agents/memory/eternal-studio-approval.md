## Current work scope

A direct owner instruction may authorize a specific, version-locked article
outside Studio when explicitly requested; this is not a general waiver of review.
Record the real source as an owner request, never a fabricated Clerk session or
approval under a reviewer's account. Preserve later Studio holds and approvals,
and require new consent for changed text or imagery.

**Why:** The owner explicitly requested a one-article release while sign-in was
unavailable, then explicitly changed that article's release to immediate.

**How to apply:** Keep such an exception single-use and audited. Do not extend it
to other posts, change their schedules or call a staged release live before
production confirms publication.

Journal photo uniqueness must be checked against other Journal entries and the
rest of the website, not just within one exported batch.

**Why:** The reviewer identified reused October photographs even though the
Journal-only file-hash check had passed. Reusing an existing site photograph
does not meet the requested unique article imagery.

**How to apply:** Check source reuse and visual similarity as well as file
hashes. A listing thumbnail for the same article is not a separate duplicate.
Use fresh review versions for replacements and preserve the old photographs
where they still belong to existing pages.

Further editorial work is limited to Eternal Journal blog posts and Eternal
emails. Care Brief material is on hold: do not create, revise, schedule or publish
it. Do not advance social, Pinterest or video material under this limited scope.

**Why:** The user explicitly narrowed work to blog posts and Eternal emails and
directed that no Care Brief material move forward.

**How to apply:** Leave existing Care Brief material intact and resume work on it
only with explicit permission. The approval requirements below still apply;
they do not authorize work on a paused channel.

Eternal Studio is the canonical place to review and approve all Journal entries,
emails and Eternal Care Brief material. All existing and scheduled entries should
be available for review there. New or revised content must receive explicit Studio
approval before public publication or email sending.

**Why:** The user created Eternal Studio for this purpose and explicitly required
approval there before content goes live. A downloadable review document or a
scheduled publication date is not a substitute for Studio approval.

**How to apply:** Make approval persistent and enforce it on publishing and sending
paths, including dated discovery surfaces. Approval must cover the material actually
released, not merely a title or browser-local status. Keep Journal and Care Brief
editorial identities separate. Do not remove already-published history without
explicit permission.

Original Journal posts dated before 2026-10-10 were already approved and posted.
Do not present their originals as awaiting first approval. A later changed
draft may still require review, separately from its original publication.

**Why:** The user explicitly confirmed this publication history after the review
queue misleadingly labeled old posts "Needs review."

**How to apply:** Use the preserved publication history for the original's label.
Do not manufacture fresh approvals, carry approval to changed drafts or restore
recruitment material that was subsequently withdrawn.

Either authorized Journal reviewer may approve an article independently; two
signatures are not required.

**Why:** The user explicitly approved extending shared approval authority to the
second designated reviewer, rather than restricting approval to the founder.

**How to apply:** Retain verified-account authorization, record the actual acting
reviewer and require version-specific consent. Do not grant approval access to
every signed-in account.

Approved Journal posts must become available automatically on their assigned dates
at a consistent daily publication time. Monitor those approved releases and send an
email alert to the designated Journal notification recipient if a scheduled post
fails to go live. This recipient may differ from the authorized Studio reviewer;
do not inherit the reviewer or Google identity-alert address automatically.

**Why:** The user explicitly required scheduled daily delivery after approval and
email notification of missed blog publications.

**How to apply:** Monitor approved posts that are actually due; deliberately held,
unapproved drafts are not failed publications. Configure the agreed daily time in
the site's local timezone and keep alert delivery separate from newsletter sending.

The user chose 6:30 a.m. Pacific Time for the daily Journal release.

**Why:** The daily release time was explicitly specified by the user.

**How to apply:** Use America/Los_Angeles local time, including daylight-saving
transitions, rather than a fixed UTC offset.

Review must cover the complete article, its photograph, contextual links and
clinical disclosures—not an excerpt or a text-only approximation.

**Why:** Approval needs to cover what families will actually see, including the
requested unique photography and in-content links.

**How to apply:** Keep article-level headings, imagery, credits and disclosures
in the review even when removing site navigation and analytics.

Studio review verification must check visible, readable content, not just item
counts or the presence of article markup.

**Why:** A populated Journal list passed a markup check while the review reader
extended beyond the visible canvas window. The reviewer still experienced a blank
screen.

**How to apply:** Check the reader after scrolling and switching views at the
actual canvas viewport size, including loaded article styles and imagery. Keep
viewport dialogs independent of the preview's page-layout constraints.

Approval of an SEO optimization plan authorizes draft editing only; it does not
approve revised article versions for publication or authorize deployment.

**Why:** The user requires separate approval of the audit/mapping before edits,
review of completed changes before production and full article approval in Studio.

**How to apply:** Keep Stage One read-only for website content and code. After plan
approval, return revised versions to Studio review; do not carry approval from an
older article version or infer publication permission from approval to optimize.

Until authoritative publisher approval is connected, stage SEO revisions in
private review documents rather than replacing public article files.

**Why:** Holding deployment is not the same as withholding a revision from a
date-based serving path. The user requires review before any version goes live.

**How to apply:** Keep original public dates, URLs and photographs intact during
review. Use revision-specific review identities so an older saved approval cannot
approve changed content, and clearly distinguish draft placements from releases.
