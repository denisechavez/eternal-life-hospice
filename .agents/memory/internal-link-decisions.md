---
name: Internal-link editorial decisions
description: Topic ownership and publication-safe recommendations across ELH pages
---

Curated recommendations should connect an article to its topic owner, not substitute
for substantive editorial links or multiply shared-footer links. Keep local city
neighbors editorially chosen; do not infer proximity from a county-wide inventory.

**Why:** The approved rollout prioritizes family answers and care/referral journeys
while preserving differentiated local content and avoiding invented coverage.

**How to apply:** Assign a narrower existing owner to new specialist/local articles;
use small unique-destination groups and preserve approved copy and neighbors.

Contextual internal links must be incorporated into the site's main sentences
and paragraphs; bottom-of-page reading groups alone do not satisfy this requirement.

**Why:** The user explicitly corrected the sitewide rollout and reiterated that
links should be encountered within the content, not just at the bottom.

**How to apply:** Link existing descriptive phrases to relevant topic-owner pages,
preserve approved clinical and business wording, and avoid automatic keyword
substitution or excessive repeated links. Obtain approval of the implementation
plan before performing this rollout.

Use the user-approved range of 2–7 contextual content links per informational
page, selected by word count and relevance, with seven as the maximum.
Existing contextual inline links count toward that limit; shared navigation,
footer links and bottom reading groups are separate.

**Why:** The user explicitly capped the proposed range at seven and specified
that word count and content should determine the selection.

**How to apply:** Treat length as a guideline, not a reason to force irrelevant
links or pad the writing. Obtain rollout approval separately from discussions
of the link-count range.

Build-time eligibility alone is insufficient for staged Journal recommendations.
Recommendations must be selected again at request time using the public publication
date, including on legacy authored pages and when cached source HTML contains links.

**Why:** Articles are staged before publication and the live site may not be rebuilt
on every publication date. Hiding a link with JavaScript is not safe discovery control.

**How to apply:** Keep dated recommendations server-rendered and revalidated. Test
both sides of publication boundaries and old cached slots when modifying linking.
