---
name: Patient guide design audience
description: Audience and visual direction for the printed patient and family guide, distinct from the web family-guide booklet.
---

The printed patient guide is for patients' families. It should feel premium and sophisticated while remaining relatable, warm and connected. Nature and professionally lit photography are welcome.

Garden is the owner's preferred direction for continued patient-guide review, not blanket approval for final printing.

**Why:** The owner said, “So far I like the direction of Garden” while requesting its download.

Use nature photographs rather than people for the guide's cover imagery. The real Aleksandra portrait in the welcome remains an explicitly requested exception. Front and back should use different, complementary scenes, not repeat the same photograph.

**Why:** After rejecting visible faces, the owner clarified “use nature photos not people no faces” and flagged repeated front/back imagery.

**How to apply:** Select nature-only sources, with a distinct scene for each exterior cover. Do not remove the separately requested founder portrait.

For the selected garden cover, keep a straight division between the plum title area and the photograph, not a feathered blend.

**Why:** The owner explicitly requested a straight line instead of feathering because it looks cleaner.

**Why:** The owner explicitly identified families as the audience and requested a stronger sense of connection than the initial cover concepts provided.

**How to apply:** Evaluate concepts as a family resource to keep at home, not a clinical sales brochure. Carry the visual identity through the inside covers. Proposed founder-message copy remains a review draft until explicitly approved; do not present drafted wording as an authentic quotation or imitate a handwritten signature.

A complete cover set has four distinct surfaces: front, inside front, inside back and outside back. Label each explicitly; an inside-back contact page is not a substitute for an outside back cover.

**Why:** The owner expected the actual back cover when reviewing the cover-and-insides concept.

Apply the four-surface requirement to every concept, not just the selected option. Alternative concepts must differ in composition and hierarchy across the set, not merely swap photographs into the same template. The founder welcome on the inside front should include the existing real founder portrait.

**Why:** The owner explicitly flagged missing outside backs in the other options and said the concepts looked the same, then requested the founder's photograph inside.

**How to apply:** Compare concepts at canvas overview scale before presenting. Confirm the front covers and interior organization differ visibly, and verify all four surfaces for each option. Use the real portrait without fabricating a likeness.

The supplied table of contents is reference for framing the founder welcome, not authorization to redesign the contents page. Frame the note as an introduction to a practical hospice handbook, including team support, comfort care, safety, privacy and patient rights, rather than a general promotional welcome.

**Why:** The owner explicitly clarified the purpose of the contents-page reference.

The selected cover prints flat at 17.5 × 11 inches: an 8.5-inch back cover, a 0.5-inch spine for inserts and an 8.5-inch front cover. Review flat artwork as two spreads: outside back on the left/front on the right, then inside front on the left/inside back on the right.

**Why:** After initially specifying 17 × 11, the owner requested a half-inch spine for inserts and explicitly chose 17.5 × 11 flat to preserve both 8.5 × 11 covers.

**How to apply:** Preserve the approved artwork when assembling spreads. Distinguish an actual-size review proof from a press-ready file; printer bleed, color requirements and final content approval still need confirmation.

Use a flattened compatibility PDF for owner review when transparency-heavy cover art is exported from Chromium.

**Why:** The owner's PDF viewer omitted the front cover's plum shading even though the same source PDF rendered correctly in Poppler. A valid PDF, matching checksum and successful local render do not prove all viewers preserve its appearance.

**How to apply:** Composite each finished spread to opaque RGB at the intended print resolution before embedding it in a compatibility copy. Verify page dimensions and visual appearance. Keep the editable/vector original separately and disclose that flattened text is no longer selectable; this does not replace press-specific color and bleed preparation.

Keep responsive screen breakpoints out of printed cover artwork.

**Why:** Chromium printing activated small-viewport rules despite the large physical sheet size, rearranging photographs and clipping the founder letter while still producing a valid two-page PDF.

**How to apply:** Limit responsive layout rules to screen media and visually inspect both rendered PDF pages, not just page count and dimensions.
