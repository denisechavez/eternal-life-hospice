Defining Professional and Household Roles

When hospice begins, it is helpful to clearly define who handles specific daily responsibilities. Our hospice nurse evaluates clinical symptoms, manages medication adjustments in partnership with the physician and provides professional care during scheduled visits. A hospice aide may also visit to assist with personal care tasks, but these visits are time-limited.

Household caregivers are essential as they provide the ongoing presence, emotional support and assistance with daily tasks that happen between our professional visits. Clear communication about what tasks fall to the caregiver and what tasks are managed by our hospice team helps prevent frustration and ensures consistent care.

We recommend holding a conversation with our intake team regarding the current level of support at home. This allows us to outline how our visit-based model supplements your efforts without overestimating the frequency of our presence or the scope of our daily involvement.

Clinical Scope and Benefit Coverage

The Medicare hospice benefit covers medical services necessary for the palliation of the terminal illness. This includes professional visits, necessary medical equipment related to the illness and medications for comfort. These services are provided based on an individualized plan developed after clinical evaluation.

It is important to understand that hospice does not provide 24-hour caregivers, housekeeping services or the costs associated with facility room and board. While we strive to support the patient's comfort, expenses outside the scope of the clinical hospice benefit are typically the responsibility of the family.

If you have questions about specific treatments or medical equipment, consult your treating clinicians. We coordinate with them to ensure that the services we provide align with the medical plan for your loved one, focusing on symptom management and quality of life.

Common questions about hospice in Downey

What is the primary role of the hospice nurse during visits?

The hospice nurse assesses the patient's symptoms, adjusts the comfort-focused care plan, provides education to caregivers and coordinates with the patient's physician. Their goal is to manage pain and other symptoms and they act as a clinical resource for the family to rely on during the course of care.

Can the hospice aide help with tasks like cooking or cleaning?

Hospice aides are specifically trained to assist with personal care tasks, such as bathing or hygiene, rather than housekeeping or cooking duties. Their visits are designed to support the physical comfort of the patient and household duties remain the responsibility of the family or private caregivers.

How should I communicate with the team about changing needs at home?

Open communication is vital. Please share any changes in the patient's condition or the family's ability to provide care directly with your assigned hospice nurse. They can help identify additional resources or adjust the care plan to ensure that the patient remains comfortable despite changing circumstances.
