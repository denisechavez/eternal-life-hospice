The Eternal Standard — Four Pillars of Care

This is the core brand architecture. All content, at some level, maps back to one of these four pillars. Use them as organizing principles for captions, videos, stories, and written posts.

Pillar 01
Clinical Confidence
The nurse who answers at 2 AM. Evidence-based protocols. ACHC accreditation. The plan of care that families trust.
Pillar 02
Guided Presence
Being with. Not disappearing after discharge. The social worker, the chaplain, the coordinator who stays.
Pillar 03
Whole-Person Comfort
Music therapy. Massage. Reiki. Aromatherapy. Pet therapy. Sound baths. The full integrative program — at no additional expense to families.
Pillar 04
Compliance-Led Care
Medicare-certified. CDPH-licensed. ACHC-accredited. Every decision made within the rules — so families never have to worry about what they're getting into.

Compliance Hard Rules — Must Follow in All Content

Rule What It Means for Content
No medical-efficacy claims for integrative therapies Never say music therapy "treats" or "heals." Say it provides comfort, supports the experience, or is offered as part of whole-person care. These therapies are not covered by Medicare as a core benefit — they are offered by Eternal at no additional expense to families.
No inducements Referral content must stay within Anti-Kickback / Stark boundaries. Highlight value and quality of care — never offer gifts, incentives, or payments to referral sources as content themes.
No Medicare "no copay" claim Say "covered under the Medicare Hospice Benefit" or "no out-of-pocket cost for eligible patients." Do not say "free" or "no cost" without qualification.
No unheld credentials Only list: Medicare-Certified, CDPH-Licensed, ACHC-Accredited. Do not list association memberships not currently held.

Acquisition-Ready,
Voice-Led

Every post serves two goals simultaneously: census growth and transferable brand equity

The fundamental rule: The brand is Eternal Life Hospice — not the founder. The founder's voice is the anchor. The founder's face is not on screen. The team humanizes the care. The institution holds the equity.

A buyer acquiring this organization in 12–18 months gets a content engine, a recognized brand, and an audience — not a person who needs to stay.

The Production Format

Voice + still image overlays. The founder (or a voiceover artist) records narration. CapCut layers it over still images, brand graphics, and text overlays. No talking head. No face. The result feels like premium documentary or editorial — not a personal vlog.

The Narration Voice

Pace: Slower than feels natural. Let sentences breathe.
Tone: Warm, measured, authoritative. Not clinical. Not corporate.
No filler: No "um," no "so," no "you know."
The pause: 1 second after key statements. It lets the sentence land.
Emotional register: Calm grief is welcome. Sentimentality is not. This is sacred work, not sad work.

The Institutional Voice

Even when the founder records the narration, the framing is always institutional:

Write This

"At Eternal Life Hospice, we…"

"Here is what our team does…"

"Families who work with us tell us…"

"She has been in hospice for eleven years. She could have practiced anywhere. She stayed."

Avoid This

"I started this because…" (use sparingly)

"My team…" (use "our team")

"When I…" (minimize first-person singular)

The Five
Visual System

Five AI-generated images. Five words. A content system that builds across nine months.

Critical naming rule: The first four elements carry "The" — they are acts, objects, forces. Stillness has no "The" — it is a state, not a thing. This distinction is intentional and must never be corrected.

The Stone · The Energy · The Word · The Touch · Stillness.

The Stone
Raw amethyst geode. Violet crystal catching window light. Grounding. What doesn't move.
The Energy
Bronze singing bowl on cream linen, warm light. Vibration in a room. Resonance long after it stops.
The Word
Gold-nib fountain pen on handwritten script. The care plan. The note. The call returned.
The Touch
Macro amber iris, warm catchlight. Being looked at and not looked away from.
Stillness.
Single candle flame in absolute darkness. A state, not a thing. Arrives last. No "The."

The Mosaic Progression — Care Brief Monthly Schedule

One element is introduced every other Care Brief month. Each issue, a new image stitches into a growing mosaic — so by Month 9, all five are complete.

The Five-Filter Test

Every photograph must pass all five before posting:

Safe. Feels like someone knows what they're doing and won't leave.

Protected. Clinical confidence — steady, not cold.

Loved. Not professionally served. Actually known.

Premium. Quality worthy of trust at the most significant moment.

Relatable. Real. Not staged. Not stock.

If an image passes all five: post it. If it fails any one of them: do not.

Black & White Photography

B&W is punctuation, not prose. Use it selectively — for the images that should be felt rather than merely seen.

Where B&W Works

Faces — honest, unmediated, powerful. A nurse with eleven years in her face, B&W, side window light.

Hands — the oldest visual language of care. Writing, resting, holding.

The amethyst in high contrast — dark exterior, luminous interior. The brand metaphor made visible.

The quiet aftermath — the empty chair, the folded blanket, the desk at end of day.

Where B&W Undercuts the Brand

Comfort therapy images — music, pet, singing bowl. Their warmth is the point. B&W makes them somber.

Nature imagery — golden hour light reads as peace. In B&W it becomes absence.

Any welcoming image — if the purpose is to invite, use color.

Feed Rhythm (Instagram, every 9)

Warm · Warm · Text-on-plum · Warm · B&W · Warm · Close-up · Warm · B&W → repeat

05 · Writing Standards Eternal Life Hospice · Social Brand Brief

The Writing
Standard

Show, don't claim. The specific is always more powerful than the general.

Banned Words & Phrases

These words have been overused in hospice marketing until they mean nothing. They are banned from this content library.

Banned Word / Phrase Why It Fails Write This Instead
Compassionate / compassion Every hospice uses it. Registers nothing — or distrust. Show the 2 AM nurse answering. Show the handwritten note. The reader feels it or they don't.
Caring / caring team Circular. A care organization calling itself caring is not information. Describe the specific act. The consistency. The presence. What actually happened.
Passionate Applies equally to hospice and pizza. Meaningless. Name what drives the specific person — what they came from, what they stayed for.
Holistic approach Medical jargon co-opted by marketing. Name the specific therapies. Name the person they help. "Music therapy. Massage. Reiki. Aromatherapy."
Journey "Your journey." "Their journey." Hollow. Name the actual thing: the season, the passage, the final chapter. Or simply: this.
Dignity (as a claim) "We provide care with dignity." Everyone says this. Show the aide who knows exactly how a patient likes their hair. Dignity is demonstrated, not announced.
Seamless Corporate. Means nothing. Describe what actually happens: same-day admission, one phone call, the medication arrives before the family asks.
World-class Claim without evidence. Name the credential (ACHC). Name the specific service. Name the specific outcome.
Loved ones Overused across all of hospice. Slightly hollow. The family. The daughter. The husband. The people in that room.

The rule: If the sentence could appear on any healthcare organization's website in America — rewrite it until it couldn't.

Show, Don't Claim — Examples

Don't Write

"We provide compassionate care."

"We treat every patient with dignity."

"We are passionate about end-of-life care."

"We offer a holistic approach."

Write This

"When a family calls at 2 in the morning, a nurse answers. Not a service. A person who can assess and act. Right then."

"Our aide knows exactly how this patient likes their hair. She has known for three weeks. She asks every time anyway — because the asking is part of the care."

"She has been in hospice for eleven years. She could have practiced anywhere. She stayed."

"Music therapy. Massage. Reiki. Aromatherapy. Pet therapy. Sound baths. A board-certified music therapist who knows your father's favorite song from 1967 and plays it at the bedside."
