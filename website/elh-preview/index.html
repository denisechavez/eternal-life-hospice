Patients and families deserve dignity, clarity, comfort and protection during one of life's most vulnerable moments. Every service we provide, every caregiver we send, every decision we make ladders back to this — four pillars that define how we care.

01
Clinical Confidence

Clinical Confidence

Behind every patient is a team of hospice-certified physicians, skilled nurses and specialist clinicians who bring hospital-grade expertise directly to your door. Advanced symptom management, respiratory care, wound care and medication coordination — handled with the precision of a specialized clinical team and the calm of people who have done this work thousands of times.

We hold Medicare certification, CDPH licensure and ACHC accreditation — the floor we build from, because families deserve more than the floor.

"Every clinical decision begins with one question: what does this patient need right now?"

Guided Presence

Families navigating end-of-life care face a cascade of decisions they were never trained for, while holding the people they love most. We stand with you in the middle of that.

Our social workers, chaplains and bereavement counselors offer guidance that is both practical and deeply human — coordinating care transitions, easing difficult conversations, holding the space when words are still forming. Every question answered. Every step explained. Never rushed.

The First 48 Hours

From the moment you call, a clear sequence begins. Federal Conditions of Participation set the standard; we treat it as a promise to your family.

Hour 0

Your call is answered

A member of our team — a person, any hour of day or night — listens, answers your first questions and begins coordinating care. You reach a real person on the first call, any hour of the day or night.

Hours 1–4

Eligibility and coverage confirmed

Our admissions team verifies Medicare, Medi-Cal or private coverage — usually the same day — and confirms there will be no surprise costs for covered services.

Same Day

Admission and first visit

Same-day admission when needed. A registered nurse conducts the initial assessment in the patient's home and the care plan begins to take shape.

Hours 24–48

The full team engages

Within the first 48 hours the interdisciplinary team — physician oversight, nursing, social work, spiritual care and aide support — is established, and medications and equipment arrive to client.

A federal standard, met as a human promise. This is the rhythm every family can count on.

Music Therapy

Many families describe the music therapy visit as the moment they saw their loved one return to themselves, however briefly.

Therapeutic Massage
Gentle, adaptive touch for comfort

Gentle, adaptive massage supports comfort and circulation, carefully modified for each patient's physical condition and comfort level. A quiet, restorative experience many describe as the most soothing part of their week.

Medicare · Medi-Cal · Most Major Plans

The Medicare Hospice Benefit — explained, simply.

One of the most comprehensive healthcare benefits in the United States, and one of the least understood. Tap below for questions answered.

For the vast majority of families enrolled in Medicare Part A, hospice care costs nothing out of pocket. No deductible. No copay for nursing visits, social work, chaplain services or bereavement counseling. No charge for medications related to the terminal diagnosis. No charge for medical equipment delivered to the home.

If your loved one has Medicare Part A and a physician's prognosis of six months or less, they are likely eligible for full coverage. Our admissions team verifies eligibility, bills Medicare directly and ensures your family never receives a surprise bill.

"No deductibles. No copays. No out-of-pocket cost for covered services."

Choosing hospice means focusing on comfort and quality of life rather than curative treatment for the terminal diagnosis. Far less changes than most families expect — conditions unrelated to the terminal diagnosis stay covered by Medicare Part B exactly as before.

Our Origin
The ancient Greeks named amethyst amethystos — clear of mind, steady in the midst of difficulty. The stone of clarity when the world becomes hard to see.

That is what we hope to be for every family — clear and steady in the hours that are hardest to see. We take our name, and our colors, from the stone: amethyst forms slowly, in darkness, across hundreds of millions of years, until it becomes something luminous.

We believe the same of this work — that what is tended gently, with patience, through the hardest hours, is often what shines the longest. It is a promise we carry from one family to the next: something older than any of us, and meant to endure.

Heart-Centered Leadership

Aleksandra Dubina

Aleksandra Dubina founded Eternal Life Hospice from a place most administrators never reach: personal knowledge of what it means to sit at a loved one's side during the final chapter of their life.

Her grandmother passed on hospice. That experience — the profound relief of attentive, dignified end-of-life care — became the reason she entered this field, and the standard she sets for every member of her team.

As a young child, Aleksandra underwent open-heart surgery, brought to the United States for advanced care. Having once been the patient — wholly dependent on the skill and compassion of others — she leads with a rare understanding of what it means to be on the receiving end of care.

With more than two decades in healthcare — eleven of those years in hospice — and credentials as an ACHC Certified Consultant, she remains a hands-on administrator who knows every patient by name.

Common Questions

Answers to what families ask us most.

Hospice is appropriate when a physician determines a prognosis of six months or less if the illness runs its normal course. Many families wish they had started sooner — earlier enrollment means more time with comfort-focused, whole-person support. If you are wondering whether it is time, that wondering is often itself a sign it is worth a conversation.

Choosing hospice is choosing a different goal — comfort, dignity and quality of life over curative treatment for a terminal illness. It is a positive, active decision to live as fully and comfortably as possible. Hospice is also reversible: a patient may return to curative care at any time.

Wherever the patient calls home — a private residence, an assisted living facility, a skilled nursing facility or a memory care community. Our team comes to the patient. Care follows them wherever they are.

Often the same day. We offer same-day admissions and 24/7 on-call nursing. When you call, we begin verifying coverage and coordinating care immediately, so support is in place when your family needs it most.

Your care comes from an interdisciplinary team and most of it happens where the patient is. A registered nurse case manager leads day-to-day care, supported by a hospice physician, certified aides for personal care, a social worker, a chaplain or spiritual-care provider when wanted and trained volunteers. They visit on a schedule built around the patient. A nurse is reachable any hour of the day or night.

Support continues around the clock. A nurse is on call 24 hours a day, every day of the year. When you call, a real person answers and a clinician helps you through whatever is happening — including a visit to the home when one is needed.

Three things: we are independent and integrative. We bring whole-person therapies into every care plan at no additional cost. And we combine the clinical depth of a nationally accredited agency with a personal, human presence that larger institutional systems find difficult to offer.
