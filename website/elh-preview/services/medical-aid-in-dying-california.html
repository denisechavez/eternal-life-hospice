Medical Aid in Dying in California

How hospice care and the California End of Life Option Act can fit together.

← Services

Medical Aid in Dying (MAID) is a serious end-of-life choice. In California, the End of Life Option Act (EOLA) creates a legal process for a qualified adult with a terminal disease to request aid-in-dying medication from an attending physician. Hospice care is a separate choice focused on comfort, support and the patient's goals.

The short answerAt Eternal Life Hospice, our team can support and coordinate care when an eligible patient is considering or pursuing Medical Aid in Dying. We do not prescribe, sell, dispense or ship aid-in-dying medication. The attending physician and other qualified professionals handle eligibility, informed consent and prescribing decisions under California law.

How Medical Aid in Dying and hospice differ

These two forms of care should not be treated as interchangeable. MAID concerns a patient's legal request for medication that the patient may choose to self-administer. Hospice is an interdisciplinary benefit and care model that addresses symptoms, comfort, family support and the practical needs of a person living with a terminal illness.

A family can ask about both without being pressured to choose either one immediately. A conversation with the patient's physicians and care team can clarify eligibility, alternatives, the plan of care and what support is available.

Eternal Life Hospice's role

Our role is to stay clear about who does what. When a patient is eligible for hospice and is exploring MAID, our team can:

  • Listen to the patient's goals and the family's questions.
  • Coordinate communication with the patient, family, attending physician and other qualified professionals involved in the plan.
  • Continue comfort-focused hospice support when appropriate and consistent with the patient's plan of care and applicable policy.
  • Provide nursing, social work, chaplaincy and other hospice support for the patient and family.
  • Explain which questions belong with the attending or prescribing physician.

Eternal Life Hospice does not operate an online pharmacy or telemedicine prescribing service. We do not provide medication instructions on this page. Any prescription, eligibility assessment, informed-consent discussion and decision about whether medication is self-administered must be addressed by the qualified professionals responsible for that process.

What California's End of Life Option Act means

California's End of Life Option Act allows an adult with a medically confirmed terminal disease to request aid-in-dying medication if the person meets the law's requirements. The law addresses matters such as California residency, decision-making capacity, medical confirmation, informed consent and the physician process. The attending physician must also discuss feasible alternatives, including comfort care, hospice care, palliative care and pain control.

The requirements are specific and can change. The official California sources below are the right place to review current forms, statutes and state guidance with the patient's physicians:

  • California Department of Public Health: End of Life Option Act
  • California Health and Safety Code, Part 1.85
  • Medical Board of California: Patient's Request for Aid-in-Dying Drug

Questions to bring to the care team

These questions can help keep the conversation clear:

  • Which physician is serving as the attending physician, and who can answer questions about eligibility?
  • What does hospice provide, and what remains with the attending or prescribing physician?
  • How will pain, breathlessness, anxiety and other symptoms be managed?
  • Who should the family call at any hour if the patient's condition changes?
  • Which current California forms, assessments and documentation apply to this patient's situation?

Start with a conversation

If you are considering hospice, or if you need to understand how hospice support can coordinate with an existing end-of-life plan, our nurse is available 24/7. A first call can focus on questions and care needs. It does not require a decision, and no patient information is needed on an initial inquiry.
