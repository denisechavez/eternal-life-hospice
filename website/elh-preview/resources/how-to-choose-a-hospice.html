How to Choose a Hospice

The questions to ask, the credentials to confirm and the signs of care that honors the whole person.

← All Resources

Choosing a hospice is one of the most consequential decisions a family makes, often under the press of a short timeline and a heavy heart. The ground is more level than it looks. Every Medicare-certified hospice covers the same core benefit. What sets one apart is how well it delivers that benefit. A handful of questions will show you the difference.

Start on level ground

Hospice is a benefit under Medicare and most insurance plans. Its core is the same wherever you go: a care team of nurses, aides, a physician, a social worker and a chaplain; the medications, equipment and supplies tied to the illness; and four levels of care that flex with need.

Those four levels run from routine care at home, to continuous nursing in a crisis, to short inpatient care when symptoms are hard to manage at home and respite that gives family caregivers rest. Because the benefit is defined, the real question is rarely what a hospice covers. It is how well each one delivers it.

AskCan this agency provide all four levels of care when we need them, including continuous nursing in a crisis and inpatient care if symptoms become hard to manage at home?

Confirm the three credentials

Three credentials show that an agency answers to outside standards, not only its own. Confirm that each is current and active:

  • Medicare certification — the agency has passed federal survey and can bill Medicare for your care.
  • A state license — in California, every hospice is licensed by the Department of Public Health (CDPH).
  • Independent accreditation — a voluntary review by an outside body such as ACHC, CHAP or The Joint Commission, held to a bar above the legal minimum.

Ask to see all three in writing. An agency that holds them and shows them plainly is meeting a high standard before you have asked the first hard question. Eternal Life Hospice holds all three: Medicare-certified, CDPH-licensed and ACHC-accredited.

Ask about the medical care

Comfort in hospice rests on strong clinical care. Ask how the medical side is run:

  • Is the medical care directed by a physician experienced in hospice?
  • How are pain and shortness of breath managed? How quickly can the plan change when symptoms shift?
  • When you call at 2 a.m., is it a nurse who can act or an answering service that takes a message? Round-the-clock availability is required. The difference is who picks up.
  • How quickly can a nurse reach the home in a crisis?
A strong sign. The agency gives you a direct number and a clear answer about who is on call, day and night.

Ask about the people and the presence

The same hospice can feel very different depending on who shows up and how often. Ask:

  • Will the same nurse and aide visit your family or does the team rotate?
  • How often will someone come? Will that increase as needs grow?
  • How quickly are calls and questions answered during the day?
  • What grief support is offered to the family? Medicare-certified hospices support families for thirteen months. Ask how they do that in practice.
A strong sign. Continuity — the same faces, returning, who already know your family’s story.

Ask about comfort for the whole person

Medicine eases the body. A strong hospice also tends to the mind and the spirit. Ask what an agency offers beyond medication:

  • Emotional and spiritual support — a social worker, a chaplain or a counselor, for the patient and the family.
  • Trained volunteers for company, respite or small daily help.
  • Care that honors your family’s culture, language and faith, with interpreters when you need them.
  • Comfort therapies such as music, gentle massage and aromatherapy, offered to ease distress and lift quality of life.
Keep in mind. These comforts do not replace medical care. They surround it, so the days hold ease, presence and dignity, not treatment alone.

Trust the first call

Some of the clearest signals come from the very first conversation.

Signs of a strong hospice

  • Answers your questions plainly, without rushing you.
  • Puts the plan, the costs and the credentials in writing.
  • Explains the Medicare benefit clearly, with no pressure to decide.
  • Can admit the same day when a family needs care now.
  • Gives you a direct line and names exactly who is on call.

Pause and ask more if

  • You cannot get a clear answer about who responds after hours.
  • You feel rushed or kept in the dark on cost.
  • The agency will not confirm its license or accreditation.
  • The visiting team keeps changing, with no one who knows your family.
  • A contract is pushed at you before you have had time to read it.
