Giving Time, Giving Presence

How hospice volunteers bring companionship and a steady hand and how you can join them.

← All Resources

A volunteer brings something medicine cannot: unhurried company, a familiar face, an afternoon where a family can breathe. Of all the gifts offered in hospice, presence is among the quietest and the most remembered.

What presence gives

So much of these days is spent waiting. Waiting is easier with company. A volunteer sits and listens. Reads a chapter aloud. Plays a little music. Keeps watch for an hour so a tired caregiver can sleep, shower or step into the sun. None of it looks dramatic. All of it matters. Families remember the person who simply showed up and stayed a while.

The shape of the work

Volunteering takes many forms. There is room for many kinds of people. Some sit at the bedside. Some help a patient record memories for the people they love. Some bring a calm creative presence or a certified animal for a gentle visit. Others give time away from the bedside entirely, with the office work that keeps care moving. The common thread is steadiness, the willingness to show up and be there.

Worth knowing. Volunteers give company and care for the spirit, never medical or hands-on clinical care. That work stays with the team. The gift here is presence. It counts as much as any of it.

If you feel called

You need no medical background, only warmth and a willingness to give time. We prepare every volunteer with training and support before a first visit. If something in this stirs you, there is a place for you here. The first step is a simple conversation.
