The Circle Around You

Leaning on the people who hold a family up and how to build that circle when you need it most.

← All Resources

No family is meant to face the end of life alone. Around every patient there is a circle, named or not, of the people who hold a family up. Naming that circle and leaning on it is one of the quiet strengths of these days.

Who makes up the circle

The circle is wider than it first seems. There is close family and there are old friends. There are neighbors who would help if only they were asked. There is a faith community, a longtime doctor, the people met along the way. And there is the care team itself, stepping in as part of the circle rather than apart from it.

How to lean on it

Help arrives more easily when it is given a shape. People want to do something; a specific task lets them. A meal on Tuesday. A ride to an appointment. An hour of company so a caregiver can rest. Saying yes is its own kind of strength. It keeps the people who love you close through a season when closeness matters most.

A gentle reminder. Accepting help is not a burden placed on others. For many, it is a gift, a way to walk beside you when words feel too small.

When the circle needs widening

Sometimes the circle is thin. That is its own kind of hard. Hospice can help fill the gaps, with trained volunteers for company and respite, with support groups and with bereavement care that walks beside a family for thirteen months after a loss. The circle can grow, even now, even here.

You do not carry this alone

Our team becomes part of your circle. We can help you widen it. Reach out and we will guide you.
