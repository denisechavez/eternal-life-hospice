The Quiet Work of Hospice Volunteers

They arrive with no clipboard and no agenda — only presence. A look at what hospice volunteers do, and why it matters so much.

← All Journal Posts
By Eternal Life Hospice · July 3, 2026 · 4 min read

Ask a family what they remember from their hospice months, and they rarely start with the medicine. They start with the people who sat with them. Among those people, often, is a volunteer — someone who came for no reason other than to be present.

Presence, not a to-do list

A hospice volunteer’s work is easy to describe and hard to overstate. They sit and talk. They read aloud. They play a favorite record. They hold a hand while a tired caregiver finally takes a shower or a nap or a walk around the block. They are the extra chair in the room, pulled up close.

What makes it powerful is exactly what makes it simple: a volunteer wants nothing from the family. There is no chart to complete, no vitals to take. That freedom lets a different kind of comfort in.

The many shapes it takes

  • Companionship — conversation, a shared silence, a game of cards, a walk in the garden.
  • Respite for caregivers — a few reliable hours so a family member can rest or run errands without worry.
  • Legacy and life-story work — helping a patient record memories, letters or a life history to leave behind.
  • Veteran recognition — honoring those who served, often alongside veteran volunteers who understand.
  • Bereavement support — a steady check-in for families in the months after a loss.
Part of the team, by design. Volunteers are not an extra. Every Medicare-certified hospice is required to include trained volunteers as part of its care team — a recognition, written into the benefit itself, that comfort is more than clinical.

Who becomes one

Our volunteers are retirees and students, nurses and neighbors, people who have walked this road with their own families and want to give something back. What they share is not a skill set but a temperament: patience, warmth and the willingness to show up and simply be there. Training and support are provided, so no one walks in unprepared.

If you feel called

If any of this stirs something in you, that feeling is worth following. Giving an hour of steady company to a family in a tender season is a small act with a long reach. We would be glad to tell you more.
