Calabasas: Clarify Hospice Responsibilities Before a Move

Before a Calabasas move, families can clarify who coordinates hospice care, how the new address affects services and what to confirm with the team.

← All Journal Posts
By The Eternal Life Hospice Team · December 10, 2026 · 2 min read

A move can change the shape of hospice care in practical ways. Before boxes are packed, families can ask who confirms the new address, who coordinates transport and whether the care setting changes. The first call begins with listening, not automatic admission, so details can be sorted early.

Illustrative photograph: Laurie Avocado / Wikimedia Commons · CC BY 2.0. Resized for display. The photograph does not depict an Eternal patient or service encounter.

Start with the new address and the current plan

Begin by sharing the address that is changing, the likely move date and whether the person will be going home, to another family house or to a care setting. Hospice availability is confirmed through intake, so service-area guidance can help frame the first conversation without assuming care will continue unchanged.

Ask how the move may affect the current plan, including visits, supplies and the person’s preferred routines. Because hospice eligibility is individualized and physician-certified, the team should be the one to clarify what stays in place and what needs a fresh review after the address change.

Match responsibilities to the setting

If the move involves a new household, ask who will help with daily caregiving, who can receive updates and where equipment can be placed. The hospice team can explain core support within the individualized plan, while family members can clarify the practical help they are able to provide in the new space.

For questions about hospice roles and family coordination, a Family Guide can support a calmer checklist. If the person values nonclinical comfort, ask whether sound bathing or end-of-life doula support is available and appropriate for the new setting, and remember that either may be declined.

Confirm transport, documents and communication

Transport planning deserves its own conversation. Ask who arranges the transfer, what information the receiving household or facility needs and which documents should travel with the person. Medicare hospice election, advance directives and decision-maker documents serve different purposes, so each should be reviewed with the right professional when questions come up.

Families who want local context can review care questions in Calabasas before calling. Keep one shared contact list, note the best phone numbers for the household and ask how the team prefers urgent questions to be handled, especially when the move happens during a busy week.

About optional integrative support

Sound bathing and end-of-life doula support are optional, nonclinical comfort offerings guided by the person’s wishes and the care plan. They do not replace licensed care. These offerings are not covered by Medicare or Medi-Cal. Eternal Life Hospice covers the expense at no additional expense to families. Ask the team about availability and fit.

Plan the move conversation before the boxes are sealed

If your family is preparing a Calabasas move, we can help you sort the practical hospice questions in a first call that starts with listening. Share the address, the setting and your concerns, then confirm the next steps together.
