When you start care with us, we establish a primary point of contact to support coordination. Our nurses and clinical staff are here to educate the new or returning caregiver on the patient's routine and comfort plan. Clinical Scope and Expectations

The hospice benefit covers clinical services directed by a physician, including nursing visits, aide support for personal care and counseling. It does not cover 24/7 private-duty nursing or household assistance. It is essential to understand that hospice is a benefit for those electing comfort-focused care over curative treatments.

We help you navigate the nuances of this benefit, explaining what is covered and where your family might need to seek outside support. This transparency is crucial for families planning for the long term. We encourage you to ask about which medicines and equipment are included under the benefit and where limited copayments or separate expenses might apply before they become an issue.

Remember that hospice is not a substitute for all healthcare needs. Your physician will help determine which existing treatments relate to the hospice plan and which should be managed separately. Our goal is to ensure you understand the boundaries of the hospice benefit so you can make decisions that honor your family's preferences and comfort goals.

Common questions about hospice in Sherman Oaks

What happens if our primary caregiver is no longer available?

If your primary caregiver's status changes, notify your hospice nurse or care team immediately. We will coordinate a meeting to update the care plan, identify a new primary contact and ensure the incoming caregiver is trained on the patient's comfort protocols and medication schedule.

Does the hospice benefit cover 24-hour caregiver support?

No. The hospice benefit is designed for clinical, visit-based support and does not include 24-hour personal care, housekeeping or continuous supervision. Families are typically responsible for arranging additional support if 24/7 monitoring or daily personal care assistance is required beyond our clinical nursing visits.

Can we change our mind about hospice care?

Yes. Hospice care is a choice that can be revoked at any time if the patient or family wishes to pursue curative treatments or no longer feels hospice is the right fit. We recommend discussing this with your physician and our team before making any changes to your care plan.
