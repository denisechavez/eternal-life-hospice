Navigating Communication in Beverly Hills

In a busy community like Beverly Hills, coordinating updates between multiple relatives, neighbors and private caregivers can become overwhelming. Establishing a clear communication plan early in your hospice journey protects the patient's privacy while keeping essential caregivers informed. Without clear boundaries, misinformation can lead to unnecessary distress during an already difficult time.

We encourage families to designate a primary point of contact for our team. This simplifies the flow of information and ensures that consistent, accurate updates reach those who need them most. By centralizing communication, you reduce the strain of fielding repeated questions and ensure that everyone is working from the same understanding of the patient's current needs.

Establishing Information Boundaries

Setting boundaries starts by identifying who needs to be involved in the patient's daily care decisions versus who simply needs periodic updates. Discuss with the patient and family members what information is considered private and how you intend to share major updates. Being intentional about these choices helps prevent later misunderstandings.

We recommend creating a shared channel or group for updates if that aligns with your family preferences. This allows you to distribute information once, ensuring that everyone receives the same message at the same time. Remember that our clinicians will only share patient information with authorized parties listed in your intake documentation.

If private caregivers or other staff are present, clarify their role in the communication chain. They may have different information needs than family members and defining their access levels early ensures that the information shared is relevant to the care they provide without infringing on family privacy.

Hospice Benefit and Communication

The hospice benefit is designed to support the patient directly. While we provide essential education to families, our primary communication remains focused on the clinical care plan and symptom management. We are not a family mediation service, though our counselors can provide guidance on managing end-of-life transitions.

Please note that hospice election does not cover all medical needs or facility charges. Many services, such as personal care beyond the hospice aide or non-related medical treatments, may remain the responsibility of the family. Clarify with our team which costs are covered under your specific benefit status.

We do not manage private-duty staff or internal facility communications. Any concerns regarding private care arrangements should be handled through your separate contracts with those providers, as our hospice team focuses exclusively on the services outlined in the clinical plan of care.

Common questions about hospice in Beverly Hills

How can I make sure my family gets consistent updates?

Designate one primary contact who will receive information from our hospice team. This individual can then disseminate updates to the rest of the family according to your established boundaries, ensuring everyone receives consistent, accurate information.

Can your team talk to my private caregiver about the patient?

We are happy to collaborate with private caregivers if they are authorized by the family. Please ensure that the hospice staff has permission to speak with them and define what types of clinical information should be shared.

What if my family disagrees on the plan of care?

Hospice supports the patient's wishes and the clinical plan established by the physician. If family members have different perspectives, we recommend a family meeting to discuss goals. Our team can participate to clarify the hospice role and medical reality.
