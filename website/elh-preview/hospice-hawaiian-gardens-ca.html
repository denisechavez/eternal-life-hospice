Essential Facts for Your First Call

When you contact us to discuss hospice in Hawaiian Gardens, avoid trying to provide an entire medical record. Instead, focus on the primary symptoms causing distress, the current medication list and the contact information for the attending physician who can discuss eligibility. This allows us to understand the current priorities effectively.

Identify who will be the primary family point of contact and whether other health aides are currently involved in daily care. Knowing how the patient communicates and what their current mobility level looks like helps our team prepare for an initial assessment visit to discuss whether hospice election aligns with your goals.

Keep a list of the most recent clinical changes or hospitalizations handy, as this helps our team understand the trajectory of care. Remember that hospice election does not necessarily mean all existing treatments must stop; we can discuss which therapies remain compatible with the comfort-focused goals of the benefit.

Understanding Hospice Coverage and Benefits

Hospice benefits provide support for comfort-focused care after a physician certifies clinical eligibility. It is important to remember that routine room and board are generally not covered by the Medicare hospice benefit. Some limited copayments may apply depending on the specific care situation and medications chosen.

While the benefit covers services related to the terminal prognosis, it does not guarantee all expenses will be paid or that care will be uninterrupted. We assist families in navigating these financial details during the enrollment process. Always consult your treating clinicians regarding treatments that fall outside of the hospice care plan.

Our team works to clarify which services are covered and which might remain the responsibility of the family. We encourage families to ask questions early if they are unsure how specific treatments or services interact with the hospice benefit during their stay in Hawaiian Gardens.

Common questions about hospice in Hawaiian Gardens

Do I need to have a complete medical record ready to call?

No. You do not need to share a full record. A simple summary of current concerns, your physician name and a list of medications is sufficient to begin our initial discussion about whether hospice might be appropriate for your loved one's needs.

Can you start care immediately upon my phone call?

Hospice care requires a clinical evaluation and a physician certification before services can begin. While we prioritize timely communication, we must follow these regulatory steps to ensure the care we provide is appropriate and safe for the patient's specific health condition.

Will a hospice nurse live in our home?

Hospice care is visit-based, meaning our team comes to your home periodically to assess needs and support your caregivers. We do not provide continuous, 24-hour staffing or private-duty nursing. Families or facility staff continue to manage the daily needs of the patient between our scheduled nursing visits.
