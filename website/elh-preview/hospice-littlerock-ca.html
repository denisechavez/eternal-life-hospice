Clarifying Medicare Hospice Coverage

Medicare hospice benefits are designed to provide specialized care for terminal illnesses, covering clinical staff visits, medications for symptom control and necessary medical equipment. However, it is important to understand that this benefit does not cover 24/7 home health aides or private-duty companionship. These services are typically paid out-of-pocket or through other insurance.

Eligibility for hospice care in Littlerock is determined by a physician's certification based on a clinical evaluation. This process ensures that the care plan is tailored to the patient's assessed needs. Be aware that not all treatments must stop upon election of hospice; we assist in determining which therapies align with the benefit and which are separate.

Regarding financial responsibility, there are no guarantees that all expenses will be covered. While hospice reduces many costs related to terminal illness, some copayments might occur. We recommend discussing financial questions openly with our intake team so your family can make decisions with a clear understanding of the coverage and your personal obligations.

Common questions about hospice in Littlerock

Does hospice provide caregivers to live with the patient?

No, hospice services are visit-based. Our clinical staff provides scheduled visits for medical and supportive needs, but we do not provide 24/7 live-in caregivers. Families must arrange for daily assistance or supervision if the patient cannot live safely on their own, ensuring they have help during non-visit hours.

How do I know if I have enough caregiver support?

If you are concerned about daily coverage, list the patient's needs: medication help, meal prep and physical assistance. If these cannot be managed by your family, you may need to hire private-duty help. Our team can discuss your current situation and help you identify where our clinical support fits.

What happens if our caregiver becomes unavailable?

We recommend having a backup plan for your caregiver network. While our clinical team will continue their scheduled visits, they cannot take over daily chores or supervision tasks. Developing a secondary network of family or friends helps maintain stability for the patient when your primary caregiver needs time away.
