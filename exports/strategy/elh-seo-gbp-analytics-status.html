Website SEO, Google Business Profile & Analytics

A plain-language status check and action list — how findable we are online, and where to focus next to grow census.

This report answers three questions: is our website fully optimized, is our Google Business Profile optimized, and where do we track our Google Analytics. The short version: the website is in excellent shape, the biggest remaining growth levers are off the website (Google Business Profile, reviews, fresh content), and Analytics is already running — we just need to make sure it’s counting the actions that matter.

1 · Website SEO

Verdict: Essentially fully optimized

For technical and on-page SEO, the site is in the top tier for a hospice of our size. Nothing major is missing. What’s already done right:

  • Titles, descriptions & canonical links — unique and well-written across all 41 pages.
  • Structured data (how Google “reads” us) — LocalBusiness / MedicalOrganization on every page, Article schema on blog & resources, FAQ schema on all 17 city pages, and breadcrumbs. This is genuinely advanced.
  • 17 city landing pages (Ventura, Thousand Oaks, Malibu, Beverly Hills, Oxnard, and more) — each with local titles, descriptions and FAQs. This is exactly how we win local searches.
  • Sitemap & robots file complete and correct; clean web addresses; security headers; even an AI-search file (llms.txt).
  • One headline per page, image alt text, and unique social-share images on every blog post.

The one small fix worth making

Two pages — the Refer page and the For Providers page — share the identical headline “Refer With Confidence,” so they slightly compete with each other in search. Giving the providers page a distinct headline (e.g. “For Hospitals, Physicians & Case Managers”) is a two-minute fix. Everything else is clean.

Honest bigger picture: on-page SEO is basically done — there isn’t much more to “optimize” on the site itself. From here, higher rankings and more calls come from off-page work: the Google Business Profile, reviews, local citations, and a steady stream of fresh content (our blog and the new monthly newsletter feed this).

2 · Google Business Profile (GBP)

Verdict: Cannot verify remotely — checklist below

Our Google Business Profile lives inside the company Google account, so it can’t be audited from the website files. But for a local hospice, GBP is arguably more important than the website — it’s what shows up in Google Maps and the local results. Here is exactly what “optimized” means. Check each item in the Google Business dashboard:

  • Claimed & verified — the profile shows as verified in the dashboard.
  • Primary category = “Hospice” — this single setting drives most of our local visibility.
  • Name, address & phone match the website exactly — Eternal Life Hospice, 4165 E Thousand Oaks Blvd, Suite 325B, Westlake Village, CA 91362, 805.953.7273. Google penalizes inconsistency.
  • Service area set to Ventura & Los Angeles County.
  • Hours — set “Open 24 hours.” Our 24/7 nurse access is a real advantage; show it.
  • Website link + a referral/contact link added.
  • Description — keyword-rich: Medicare-certified hospice serving Ventura & LA County, plus the four pillars of care.
  • Photos — logo, office, and team. Listings with photos get far more calls.
  • Reviews — the single biggest ranking factor. Actively ask satisfied families and partners, and reply to every review.
  • Google Posts — post regularly. Our blog and newsletter content is perfect source material.
  • No duplicate listings for the same address.

3 · Google Analytics — where to track it

Verdict: Installed & running

Google Analytics (GA4) is already live on the site — measurement ID G-JRLYCRC48G, running across our pages. Here’s how to view it:

  1. Go to analytics.google.com.
  2. Sign in with the Google account that owns the property — the same Google world as our Business Profile. (Add teammates like Aleksandra under Admin → Property Access Management.)
  3. Select the Eternal Life Hospice property (G-JRLYCRC48G).

The four reports that matter most for census

  • Realtime — who’s on the site right now (a quick way to confirm it’s working).
  • Acquisition → Traffic acquisition — where visitors come from: Google search, our Business Profile, direct, social. Shows what’s working.
  • Engagement → Pages and screens — which pages people actually read (city pages, blog posts).
  • Engagement → Events — the actions people take on the site.
Two honest caveats. Analytics only shows data from when it was installed forward (no history before that), and full reports can lag a day or two — Realtime is instant.
The most important number may not be tracked yet. By default GA4 counts visits and page views, but it won’t specifically tell us “someone submitted the referral form” or “someone tapped the phone number” unless those are set up as conversion events. For a hospice, those are the numbers that prove our marketing is growing census — worth setting up.

Recommended next actions

In priority order

  1. Confirm the Google Business Profile is fully optimized using the checklist above — this is the highest-leverage step for local visibility and calls.
  2. Start collecting reviews from families and referral partners, and reply to each. Reviews move GBP rankings more than anything else.
  3. Set up conversion tracking in Analytics (referral-form submissions and phone taps) so we can measure real leads, not just traffic.
  4. Make the two-minute website fix — a distinct headline on the For Providers page.
  5. Keep publishing — the monthly newsletter and blog posts feed both SEO and Google Posts.
