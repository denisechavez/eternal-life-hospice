2. Hospice in assisted living and skilled nursing

Replace the Coverage paragraph

The Medicare Hospice Benefit covers eligible hospice services in a care community. It generally does not cover ongoing facility room and board. Short-term inpatient or respite care arranged by the hospice follows different coverage rules. Medi-Cal or other coverage may help with room and board depending on eligibility and the setting. Eternal reviews the family's coverage and potential costs before care begins.

Replace the room-and-board FAQ answer

The Medicare Hospice Benefit generally does not pay for ongoing facility room and board. It may cover a short-term inpatient or respite stay arranged by the hospice when the applicable requirements are met. Medi-Cal and other coverage have different rules. Ask Eternal to review the facility charges and your coverage before care begins.

Billing review required, including California Medi-Cal nursing-facility arrangements. Do not imply that every family must pay privately or that Medi-Cal pays in every care setting.

3. Levels of Hospice Care

Replace the introduction

Medicare defines four levels of hospice care. Routine home care usually involves scheduled visits. When needs change, the hospice team assesses whether another level is appropriate under Medicare guidelines and coordinates the care setting.

Replace the line below “Three levels beyond routine home care”

These levels address specific needs. The team reassesses the appropriate level and care setting as needs change.

Replace the Continuous Home Care description

Continuous Home Care is a short-term level for periods of crisis when Medicare criteria are met. It is provided in an eligible home setting and consists mainly of nursing care. The hospice team determines whether this level is appropriate and available for the patient's situation.

Replace the Inpatient Respite Care description

Inpatient respite care gives the usual caregiver a short rest while the patient continues to receive hospice care. The hospice arranges an occasional stay in an approved inpatient facility for up to five consecutive days.

Replace the return-home FAQ

What happens after inpatient care?
When the inpatient level is no longer needed, the team reassesses the patient's needs and coordinates the next appropriate level and setting. Returning to a previous home or care community depends on the individual situation.

Confirm current inpatient and respite arrangements before describing them as services Eternal can arrange. Continuous Home Care must not be presented as available in every residence or as general around-the-clock caregiving.

4. 24/7 Nurse Access

Replace the eyebrow

24/7 hospice nurse access by phone across Ventura and Los Angeles counties

Replace the introduction

24/7 nurse access gives enrolled patients and families a way to reach hospice nursing support by phone at any hour. The nurse assesses the concern, guides next steps and arranges a visit when clinically needed under the plan of care. Phone access does not mean a nurse stays continuously at the bedside.

Replace “What happens when you call”

A hospice nurse discusses what has changed, provides guidance and coordinates the next step under the patient's plan of care. Keep 805.953.7273 and the team's written contact instructions close by.

Propose replacing the 911 FAQ with a contact-planning question

Who should we call when symptoms change?
Follow the contact and emergency instructions in the patient's care plan. The hospice nurse can guide the response to hospice-related concerns. Ask the team to explain what to do for urgent concerns and emergencies before a situation arises.

Clinical review of emergency instructions is mandatory. This draft is not an emergency protocol and must not be used to discourage emergency services. Confirm who answers after hours and how clinically necessary night visits are staffed before retaining those promises elsewhere in the brief.

5. In-Home Hospice Care and Hospice Evaluation

Replace unconditional same-day admission statements

Admission timing depends on physician-confirmed eligibility, the patient's needs and care coordination. Eternal explains the next steps and anticipated timing during the evaluation.

Replace “How quickly can care begin?”

How quickly can care begin?
The team reviews eligibility and care needs, then explains the earliest appropriate start date. Ask about current availability when you call.

If same-day admission is confirmed, propose instead: “Same-day admission may be available when clinically appropriate, after physician-confirmed eligibility and care coordination.” Do not present this alternative as approved. Apply the selected wording consistently to the timeline and any longer reference text.

Replace the evaluation commitment FAQ answer

An evaluation provides information and does not enroll a patient in hospice. The patient or authorized representative decides whether to elect hospice after eligibility and care options are explained.

Keep the nurse-assessment and physician-eligibility distinction. Do not replace the existing general hospice or first-48-hours resource pages.

6. Coverage panel and benefit terminology

Proposed shared heading

Understanding your hospice coverage

Proposed shared body for clinical service pages

Hospice coverage depends on eligibility and the rules of your Medicare, Medi-Cal or private insurance plan. Eternal reviews benefits, coverage arrangements and potential costs before care begins.

Keep Medicare and Medi-Cal references subject to eligibility. Hold Kaiser Permanente, L.A. Care, AltaMed, Veterans Affairs and “LOAs accepted” chips until the actual arrangement, service scope and current status are confirmed. This mixed list must not imply that every named organization is an insurer or that displaying its name establishes a contract.

For covered medications, supplies and equipment, propose using “related to the terminal illness and related conditions, as included in the hospice plan of care” rather than only “related to the hospice diagnosis.” Clinical and billing reviewers should approve the wording consistently.

Do not imply that Medicare or Medi-Cal covers optional sound bathing or end-of-life doula support. Eternal-funded integrative support must remain clearly separate from the hospice insurance benefit.

7. Bereavement Support

The source brief identifies thirteen months as an Eternal-specific service promise awaiting final sign-off. Do not silently change it to twelve months or describe thirteen months as a federal minimum.

Proposed introduction while the duration remains unresolved

Bereavement support is for family members and close loved ones affected by the loss of a person in hospice care. The team discusses available support, how it is provided and how long it continues.

Proposed interim meta description

Bereavement support for hospice families, with information about grief resources, check-ins and support before and after a loss.

Keep the original thirteen-month copy as an explicitly pending alternative if it is confirmed. Also confirm in-person support, children's support, memorial activities and check-in practices rather than inferring them from general requirements.

Use a page-specific panel instead of the general insurance panel

Understanding bereavement support
Ask the team which bereavement services are included in the hospice bereavement plan. Outside counseling or community programs may have their own eligibility requirements, availability and fees.

Do not imply that independent grief counseling is automatically paid for by a listed health plan.

8. Respiratory Care and outcome wording

Proposed Respiratory Care introduction

Respiratory support within hospice is for eligible patients with COPD, lung disease or heart failure who experience shortness of breath. The team develops a comfort-focused plan that may include medications, equipment coordination and guidance for caregivers. Care is provided at home or in the patient's care community.

Proposed breathlessness FAQ answer

The hospice team assesses the cause and the person's needs. The plan may include medication, positioning, airflow and other measures appropriate to the patient. The nurse explains what to try and when to call for further help.

Proposed Heart Failure subline

Comfort-focused support for breathing changes and a clear plan for what comes next.

These changes avoid suggesting general pulmonary treatment, universal oxygen eligibility or guaranteed fewer emergencies. Equipment availability and clinical claims still require confirmation.
