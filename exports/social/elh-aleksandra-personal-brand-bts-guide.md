# Elevating Aleksandra + Behind-the-Scenes Content Guide
## Personal Brand Strategy for a Hospice Founder

*Companion to: elh-organic-growth-playbook.md*

---

## Why This Matters More Than Any Brand Post

People do not follow hospices. They follow people.

Aleksandra is the story. She is the reason this hospice exists. Her face, her voice, her conviction — those are the things that cannot be copied, automated, or outsourced. Every post where she shows up as a human being (not a logo) does more for trust, referrals, and census than any graphic or caption.

The goal: make Aleksandra **the known expert on death, dignity, and hospice excellence in Southern California.** When a discharge planner in Ventura County thinks "who do I trust with this patient?" — the answer should be her name.

---

## Aleksandra's Personal Brand Positioning

**Who she is publicly:** Founder and CEO of Eternal Life Hospice. Certified Hospice Administrator. A woman who chose to build something in the space most people run from — because she believes death deserves more care, not less.

**Her lane:** Where clinical expertise meets deep humanity. She is not a marketer. She is not a cheerleader. She is someone who has sat in rooms most people never enter, and who speaks from that place with warmth and authority.

**Her voice:** Warm. Direct. Unhurried. She does not perform grief. She does not perform optimism. She tells the truth plainly.

**Her signature perspective:**
- Death is a part of life — not a failure, not a defeat
- Hospice is a continuation of care, not an ending
- Every family deserves this level of attention
- The details are how love is expressed in clinical care

## The Three Aleksandra Content Formats

### 1. "The Founder's Perspective" — 60–90 sec talking head
**When:** 2–3x per week, the core format
**How:** Phone, window light, clean background, no teleprompter
**Tone:** Like she's talking to one person she trusts
**What she talks about:**
- Something she witnessed this week that moved her (without identifying patients)
- A clinical or operational decision and why she made it
- A question families ask that she wishes she could answer more widely
- Something that made her proud of her team
- Something broken in hospice care, and how they do it differently

**Opening line patterns (never start with "Hello" or "Today I wanted to share"):**
- "Something happened this week that I can't stop thinking about."
- "The most common question I get is ______. Here's the honest answer."
- "I started this hospice because ______. This week reminded me why."
- "Most people don't know that ______."
- "A family called me last week and asked ______."

---

### 2. "Behind the Scenes" — 15–45 sec B-roll or POV
**When:** Daily or near-daily for Stories; 2–3x per week for Reels/TikTok
**How:** Phone camera, unstaged, real
**Tone:** Inviting, not performative. "Here's what this actually looks like."
**What to film:** (see full list below)

---

### 3. "In Conversation" — longer form, 3–8 min
**When:** 1x per week for YouTube; cut-downs for LinkedIn and TikTok
**Format:** Aleksandra at her desk, or in conversation with a team member
**Topics:**
- Interview-style with her RN, social worker, or chaplain
- "What I've learned in [X] years of hospice"
- A conversation with a caregiver about what the experience was like
- Deep-dive on a clinical topic (timely referrals, the four levels of care)

## Specific Series Concepts (Recurring Formats = Algorithm Reward)

Platforms favor creators who post consistent series. Here are four to start:

---

### Series 1: "Ask Aleksandra"
**Format:** 60-sec answer to one question, posted weekly
**How it works:** Post on Monday: "What's the one question you've always wanted to ask about hospice? Drop it below." Film answers to the best comments as separate posts Wednesday–Friday.
**Examples:**
- "Can a hospice patient go to their grandchild's graduation?" (Yes — with coordination)
- "What if we want to stop hospice and try treatment again?" (Yes — the benefit can be revoked)
- "Will a nurse actually come in the middle of the night?" (Yes — this is the benchmark)
- "How long does the average patient have when they start hospice?" (No way to answer that — here's the honest truth)

**Platform home:** TikTok (comment-reply-with-video feature), Instagram Stories (question sticker)

---

### Series 2: "The Detail That Matters"
**Format:** 15–30 sec close-up of one specific operational detail, with brief VO or caption
**How it works:** Film one small, intentional detail from the office or care process each week. The "detail" is the story.
**Examples:**
- The labeled shelf in the supply room
- The handwritten family note
- The morning whiteboard
- The intake packet organized by tab
- The care plan printed and signed (no patient names visible)
- Medications confirmed before delivery

**Caption template:** "Most families never see this. Here's what happens before we ever ring your doorbell."
**Platform home:** Instagram Stories, Instagram Feed, TikTok

---

### Series 3: "The People Behind the Care"
**Format:** 60–90 sec mini-interview with one team member per week
**How it works:** Aleksandra films a short conversation with a team member. Two questions max. Real answers. No scripts.
**Questions to ask:**
- "What's the hardest part of this job — and why do you keep doing it?"
- "What's one thing families are always surprised to learn?"
- "Tell me about a moment that stayed with you." (without identifying details)
- "What would you want your own family to know about hospice?"

**Platform home:** TikTok, LinkedIn, YouTube (full version)

---

### Series 4: "From the Founder's Desk"
**Format:** Weekly 2–3 min LinkedIn post, with companion short-form video
**How it works:** Aleksandra writes (or speaks) one honest reflection per week. Clinical, human, specific. Not marketing.
**Topic bank:**
- "What I learned from this week's admissions"
- "A question that made me rethink how we explain our care"
- "Why I care about ACHC accreditation personally"
- "What happened when I sat in on a care plan meeting"
- "The phone call I almost didn't make — and why I'm glad I did"
- "What the families who call us early have in common"
- "The hardest conversation in hospice — and how we have it"

**Platform home:** LinkedIn (primary), cross-post to Facebook, pull quotes for Instagram

## What NOT to Film (Compliance + Ethics)

- Never film a patient, even in passing, without documented written consent
- Never film a family member in crisis or emotional distress
- Never show a patient name, date of birth, diagnosis, or address — even partially
- Never film medications with patient labels visible
- Never film inside a patient home without specific written consent from family AND patient (if capacity allows)
- Care plans, charts, and intake documents must have all identifying information out of frame or covered
- If in doubt: film the hands, the object, the space — not the paperwork

**The visual rule:** If there's text in frame, confirm it contains zero PHI before posting.

## How to Make the Office Look Great on Camera

You don't need a video crew. You need good light and a steady hand.

### Lighting (the #1 thing)
- Film near windows — natural light makes every space look warmer and more real
- Avoid overhead fluorescent lighting — it flattens faces and looks clinical
- Golden hour (7–9 AM or 4–6 PM) makes everything beautiful
- If you have ring lights, use them for Aleksandra's talking-head videos, not for room shots

### Phone settings
- Film in 4K if your phone supports it — you can always reduce quality but not increase it
- Stabilize: lean against a wall, prop on a surface, use a $15 phone tripod
- For slow pans and detail shots: move slowly — twice as slow as you think is slow enough
- For close-ups of details: tap the subject on your screen to focus, then film

### What makes the space look intentional
- Clear surfaces before filming — one or two meaningful objects, not clutter
- Brand colors in frame when possible — a plum accent pillow, a cream-colored notepad, a gold pen
- Real work in the background — a lit computer, an open binder, a phone — signals active care
- Plants or natural elements make any office feel warmer

## The Content That Will Build the Deepest Trust

In order of impact:

1. **Aleksandra's Why** — why she started this, in her own words, on camera, once a month
2. **Team member moments** — real people, real answers, not scripted
3. **The detail shots** — the things families never see but should know about
4. **Real family letters** (with permission, names covered) — the most powerful trust signal
5. **The morning huddle** — showing that every patient is thought about every day
