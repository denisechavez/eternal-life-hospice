The person holding the vigil needs holding too.

You arrived, you stayed, and you are still here. In the weeks after a hospice enrollment, we watch what caregivers carry — the quiet monitoring, the interrupted nights, the weight of being the one who holds everything together. This issue is for you.

What sustaining yourself actually looks like

Hospice care is built around a patient — but the family around them is never invisible to us. Our social workers, nurses, chaplains and aides are part of your support, not just your loved one’s. If you are wearing down, that is something we want to hear, not something to push through alone.

A few things we have learned from the families we walk alongside: accepting help is not defeat. Naming what you feel — to a friend, to our social worker, or simply to yourself — reduces its weight. And the nurse who calls to check on your loved one can just as easily check on you. You only have to say so.

Practical things that help: keeping a short list of what someone else could do this week and sharing at least one item on it. Eating at least one real meal a day. Stepping outside, even for five minutes, when someone else is in the room. These are not small gestures — they are how caregivers endure.

The support described here is offered as practical guidance. Our clinical team can help connect you with the right member of your care team for personalized support.

This week’s small practice

Write down three things someone else could do for you this week — and share at least one of them out loud. Our team has seen this single act change the shape of a month. If you’re not sure who to ask, start with us: a social worker is part of every plan of care, and their time is yours.
