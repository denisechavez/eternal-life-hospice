Audit the current information architecture, page hierarchy, navigation, internal linking, URL structure, and homepage user experience for the Eternal Life Hospice website.

IMPORTANT:
This is an analysis and recommendation task only.

Do not edit files.
Do not change routes.
Do not redesign pages.
Do not remove content.
Do not deploy anything.
Do not alter the live website.

The purpose of this audit is to determine how the existing website should be reorganized to:

1. Improve user experience
2. Reduce homepage length and cognitive overload
3. Create clearer pathways for families and healthcare professionals
4. Strengthen Google’s understanding of the website hierarchy
5. Improve internal-link authority distribution
6. Reduce topic overlap and keyword cannibalization
7. Support qualified hospice referrals and admissions
8. Improve the effectiveness of future Google Ads landing pages
9. Preserve all valuable existing content rather than deleting it unnecessarily

BUSINESS PRIORITY:

Eternal Life Hospice needs qualified referrals and admissions. Recommendations should prioritize conversion and referral pathways—not merely rankings or traffic.

PRIMARY AUDIENCES:

1. Families urgently considering hospice
2. Healthcare professionals referring a patient
3. Families researching hospice before an immediate need
4. Community and professional visitors learning about Eternal’s integrative care model

PRIMARY CONVERSION ACTIONS:

- Call for hospice guidance
- Request hospice care
- Refer a patient
- Read or download the Family Guide
- Contact Eternal Life Hospice

PART 1 — INVENTORY THE CURRENT WEBSITE

Inspect the complete project and produce a current page inventory showing:

- Page name
- URL
- Route file or component
- Current navigation location
- Primary audience
- Current apparent purpose
- Primary CTA
- Approximate content depth
- Whether the page is indexable
- Whether it is included in the sitemap
- Number or approximate volume of internal links pointing to it
- Pages it links to
- Whether it appears duplicated, overlapping, isolated, thin, or strategically important

Include all:

- Main pages
- Resource pages
- Provider pages
- Referral pages
- Service-area pages
- City pages
- Integrative modality pages
- Family Guide pages
- Careers and volunteer pages
- Legal and utility pages
- Any orphaned or legacy routes

PART 2 — AUDIT THE HOMEPAGE

Analyze every homepage section in its current order.

For each section, state whether it should:

- Remain on the homepage
- Be shortened
- Be converted into a navigation card or teaser
- Move to a dedicated page
- Be combined with another section
- Be removed because it is redundant
- Remain unchanged

Identify:

- Repeated messages
- Competing calls to action
- Sections that appear too early or too late
- Information families need immediately
- Information referral professionals need immediately
- Content that belongs on a supporting page
- Any trust information that should remain above the fold
- Mobile scrolling or usability concerns

Recommend a revised homepage structure that is approximately 30–40% shorter while retaining the strongest trust and conversion content.

The recommended homepage must answer these questions quickly:

1. What does Eternal Life Hospice provide?
2. Where does Eternal provide care?
3. Why should a family or provider trust Eternal?
4. What makes Eternal different?
5. What should the visitor do next?

PART 3 — PROPOSE THE IDEAL SITE ARCHITECTURE

Create a proposed sitemap using a clear parent-and-child hierarchy.

Evaluate whether the primary navigation should use categories such as:

- Hospice Care
- For Families
- Healthcare Professionals
- Integrative Care
- Service Areas
- About
- Resources
- Contact

Do not assume these labels are correct. Recommend the strongest labels based on the actual content and user journeys.

For every recommended page, provide:

- Navigation label
- Page title
- Suggested URL
- Parent section
- Primary audience
- Primary search intent
- Main conversion goal
- Existing content that should move there
- Whether the page already exists
- Whether it needs revision, consolidation, or creation
- Whether it belongs in the main navigation, dropdown, footer, or contextual links only

PART 4 — CREATE USER JOURNEYS

Map the shortest logical pathway for each visitor type.

Journey A:
A family needs hospice urgently.

Journey B:
A physician, case manager, discharge planner, social worker, skilled nursing facility, or assisted living professional wants to refer a patient.

Journey C:
A family is unsure whether it is time for hospice.

Journey D:
A visitor wants to understand Medicare hospice coverage.

Journey E:
A visitor wants to understand Eternal’s integrative hospice care and comfort modalities.

Journey F:
A visitor wants to confirm whether Eternal serves their city.

Each journey should reach its primary conversion action in no more than three meaningful clicks from the homepage.

PART 5 — TOPIC AND KEYWORD OWNERSHIP

Identify which existing page should be the primary authority page for each topic:

- Hospice care in Ventura and Los Angeles Counties
- Hospice eligibility
- When it is time for hospice
- Medicare hospice coverage
- What happens during the first 48 hours
- Family hospice guidance
- Healthcare professional referrals
- Integrative hospice care
- Comfort therapies
- Sound bath
- Service areas
- Individual cities
- Careers
- Volunteer opportunities

Identify pages that currently compete for the same topic.

For every overlap, recommend whether to:

- Keep both pages with separate search intent
- Consolidate them
- Shorten one and link to the authoritative page
- Redirect one page
- Reposition one as supporting content

Do not recommend redirects until the current URL value, backlinks, traffic risk, and content purpose have been considered.

PART 6 — INTERNAL LINKING ARCHITECTURE

Recommend a hub-and-spoke internal-link structure.

At minimum, evaluate these possible hubs:

- Hospice Care
- Family Resources
- Healthcare Professionals
- Integrative Hospice Care
- Service Areas

For each hub, list:

- Supporting pages
- Required links from the hub
- Required links back to the hub
- Contextual cross-links
- Recommended descriptive anchor text
- Pages currently receiving too few internal links
- Pages receiving excessive or unfocused links
- Orphaned or near-orphaned pages

Ensure that important pages are reachable using standard crawlable HTML links and are generally no more than three clicks from the homepage.

PART 7 — SERVICE-AREA ARCHITECTURE

Evaluate the current city and regional page structure.

Recommend whether the strongest organization is:

Option A:
Service Areas → Ventura County / Los Angeles County → Individual cities

Option B:
Service Areas → Regional hubs such as Conejo Valley, San Fernando Valley, South Bay, and individual cities

Option C:
A hybrid regional and county structure

Consider:

- User clarity
- Duplicate content risk
- Google local relevance
- Internal linking
- Breadcrumb structure
- Navigation complexity
- Whether city pages are genuinely unique
- Whether any city or regional pages compete with one another

Do not recommend creating additional city pages unless they can contain meaningful unique local content and serve a real business objective.

PART 10 — IMPLEMENTATION PLAN

Create a one-day implementation plan divided into safe stages:

Stage 1:
Create backups or a restore point and document current routes.

Stage 2:
Update navigation and page hierarchy.

Stage 3:
Shorten the homepage by moving detailed content to existing supporting pages.

Stage 4:
Create or refine any essential hub pages.

Stage 5:
Improve internal links and breadcrumbs.

Stage 6:
Update metadata, canonicals, sitemap, and structured data only where required.

Stage 7:
Test desktop, tablet, mobile, forms, phone links, referral pathways, redirects, and build integrity.

Clearly separate:

- Architecture changes
- Content moves
- Copy changes
- Technical SEO changes
- Visual design changes
- New page creation
- Redirects
